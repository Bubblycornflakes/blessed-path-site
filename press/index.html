Describing it

Use these as they are, or trim them. They're written to be accurate.

In one line

Gracious Path is a free iPhone and iPad app for gratitude and small acts of kindness, with a desert garden that grows as you go. No account, no ads, and nothing leaves your phone.

Short

Each day, Gracious Path asks for three things you're grateful for and suggests one small, practical kindness to do, like holding the lift for someone or calling a relative you haven't spoken to in a while. Every kindness you complete grows a hand-drawn desert garden that never shrinks on quiet days. There's no account and no tracking: everything stays on your phone.

Longer

Gracious Path is a quiet daily companion for gratitude and kindness. Each day it asks for three things you're grateful for and offers two small, practical kindnesses from a library of 133 ideas: one is the day's goal, the second is there if the day allows. You can swap a suggestion, or write in something kind you did yourself.

Every completed kindness grows a hand-drawn desert garden, from a seed in the sand to an oasis. There are no streaks to break: quiet days never shrink it. A calm calendar keeps every day, each week brings a short reflection, and widgets put today's kindness and a daily thought on the Home Screen and Lock Screen.

The app is private by design. There is no account, no server, no analytics and no advertising. Notes are stored only on the phone, and the widgets never show them. It's free, with an optional tip to the developer that unlocks nothing.

What makes it different

It asks you to do something

Most gratitude apps stop at writing. Gracious Path adds one small, real kindness to do each day, chosen to suit your time, budget and where you are.

It's private by design

No sign-in and no server at all. Notes never leave the phone, so there is nothing to collect, sell or leak.

It's gentle

No streak counters and no guilt. The garden only grows, and the optional reminder steps aside once the day's goal is met.

The story

Gracious Path was made by one person, Aslam, who isn't a programmer. The idea was simple: one calm app that nudges you to be a little kinder each day, with no account, no ads and no streak counter to make you feel bad on a quiet day. Nothing like it existed, so Aslam built it.

The app also includes a page of sixteen humanitarian organisations, with links to their own websites, for days when someone wants to do more. Gracious Path takes nothing from those links and isn't endorsed by any of them.

I wanted one calm thing on my phone that nudged me to be a bit kinder each day. I couldn't find it, so I built it.Aslam, maker of Gracious Path
